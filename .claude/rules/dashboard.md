# Dashboard rules

The dashboard, "Who's eating the farmer's peso?", is five pages for a reader who knows nothing of farm prices and doesn't follow the news (ADR 25): the story, who got caught, check your food, the short version, and how it's built. Pages 1 and 2 read as a mystery (ADR 24): a question the reader feels, who stands between farm and store, growing and sticky, then the proof that it's every food, who got caught and what can be done. Pages and sections are in `GLOSSARY.md` → Dashboard; `nav.py` lists the pages; layout is in `src/dashboard/views.py` and `how.py`. Load `developing-with-streamlit` and `dashboard-design` before changing it.

## Scope
- **Five pages under `st.navigation(position="top")`, each ending on an `st.page_link` to the next** (`views.NEXT`), with the caption row (`masthead`) and the PSA caption (`footer`) on every page. Page 1 carries the whole finding in about 90 seconds on a phone; a new question becomes a section only when it changes what a reader concludes, otherwise it goes in "Check your food" or a `help=` tooltip.
- **Word budget: each section is its headline, at most one line under it and at most one so-what line, no paragraphs.** Detail goes in a tooltip or page 1's closed Sources expander, which holds the citations for page 1's hand-written facts. `test_pages.py` counts the lines under each headline on pages 1 and 2.
- **A data section is one or two big numbers (`hero`), one computed headline, one line beneath it, a chart and one so-what line under the chart** (`so_what`, computed like the headline: why the chart matters to a shopper). Page 1 keeps only the hook's and the ratchet's charts; the per-food charts sit on page 2 under subheaders, growth since 2018 as a dumbbell whose labels give both percents. A grey caption "Part N of M · Name" (`views.CHAPTERS`) sits above each section on pages 1 and 2. A context section (the chain, who got caught, what can be done) may be text only; it reads as bordered cards with grey badges, not a run of paragraphs. The reader acts, not only reads: the hook hides the store price, and any big number, until they guess it (`views.HOOK_GUESS`), and the receipt is their own basket, the example until they change it (ADR 26). The ratchet is a still example (`views.RATCHET_MOVES`), not a toy. The headline is a full sentence a newcomer can repeat.
- **The short version is bullets, no charts:** each a bold finding with at most one number, computed live (`data.short_version`), then a plain "So …" for the reader or the farmer.
- **Shares in centavos, price moves in pesos, a change in percent:** "X centavos of every peso" for a share (as USDA's Food Dollar says "cents per dollar"), "₱X" for a real price or price move ("drops ₱10"), "26%" for a year's change or growth since 2018, where meat's live-animal price can't be set peso for peso against a cut (ADR 18, ADR 23).
- **Meat is percent only; fish is by quarter.** Meat never gets a farmer's share or a peso gap. A fish row's month is its quarter's first, so name its period with `views.when` ("July–September 2026").
- **Every sentence opens with a word.** Lead with the noun and put the number after it ("Most foods, 14 of 16, …"), so "₱10. 14 of 16" never reads as "10.14". `test_pages.py` checks every header, line and caption.
- **Plain:** stock Streamlit widgets, `st.echarts_chart` for the data sections' charts (ADR 17, 18), and Altair for "Check your food". ECharts options are JSON only (no JS formatters, `custom` series or maps) with `theme="streamlit"`, and a `key` so a rerun doesn't replay them. Their entry animation (`views._once`) is 0.8 s, once, never looping, and the finished frame must make the point without the motion. The only CSS is in `app.py` (the `dashboard-no-css` hook keeps `<style>` out of `views.py`): tabular digits, the big numbers' size (keys `hero_<section>_<i>`), the scroll fade-in on `reveal_<name>` containers, which honours reduced motion, and the chain's arrows (`chain_arrow_<i>`), turned to point down where a phone stacks the columns. Check motion with Playwright frames taken mid-scroll: a screenshot shows only the finished page. The only JavaScript is in `theme.py`: the light/dark button and the light default, both writing Streamlit's own theme key in localStorage; check after a Streamlit upgrade that the button still flips the page and a fresh dark-system visit opens light.
- **Read in order, the headlines tell the story.** Each is short enough to say aloud; the line beneath it says how to read the chart in plain words, never a colour key like "Amber: X. Grey: Y."
- Each caveat sits in the `help=` tooltip of the headline it qualifies. A tooltip is one line saying what the number is, plus at most three short bullets for the caveats that change how to read it, so a newcomer takes it in at a glance. A number shows once on the page: when two sections would compute the same quantity differently, only one shows it. A visible caption holds only the month, the source, a part label or how to read the chart. An empty state is a caption.
- **Blame comes only from the public record** (`suspects.csv`): rulings, findings and charges, plus allegations labelled "alleged"; opinions stay out. Each is worded as its source words it, names no private person, and sits on page 2, rulings first. Our prices show a pattern, never a culprit.
- **Causes may sit in the main text**, fair ones (costs) and unfair ones (cartels, hoarding), each cited, never inferred from our prices.

## Honest
- Numbers come from the dbt marts (ADR 9). `data.py` only filters, scales and ranks them, and every helper has a test in `tests/test_dashboard_data.py`.
- Data headlines and numbers are computed from the data on each release. Context text is written by hand, and every fact in it carries a source name, URL and date (`src/dashboard/context.py`), shown on its card or in page 1's Sources expander.
- Plain words on screen: "farm price", "store price", "the gap", "inflation". "Farmgate", "retail", "middle's cut", "CPI", "pass-through", "verdict", "suspicious" and "staple" are code terms.

## Colour
One palette, the same value in light and dark (ADR 19, ADR 21). Blue (`FARM`) is the farmer; amber (`AMBER`) is the middle: traders' part of the peso, the gap between farm and store, the ratchet's climb, the wholesale line. The store price is ink, the theme's text colour: leave a store series uncoloured as the second uncoloured series in its chart (`views.STORE_SLOT`), and the theme's `chartCategoricalColors` gives it the ink in each mode, since Python learns the theme a rerun late. Stores' part of the peso is plain; everything else is grey (`MUTED`). The theme's `blueColor`/`blueTextColor` equal `FARM` and `orangeColor`/`orangeTextColor` equal `AMBER`, so `:blue[...]` and `:orange[...]` words, the big numbers (`views.paint`) and the bars always match; a test holds them, and the palette's first three slots (blue, ink, amber), together. Each headline colours its key words like its big number (plain, never `**bold**`, which renders lighter inside a header), and the line under it names each colour, so colour never carries meaning alone. Every role colour keeps at least 3:1 on both backgrounds, and text drawn on a blue bar is white. ECharts text left uncoloured takes the theme's ink.

## Data
- All data access goes through `src/dashboard/data.py`. The page reads `farmer_share_monthly`, `food_verdicts`, `price_changes_yearly`, `growth_since_2018`, `change_only_monthly` and `seasonal_baseline`. `dbt/seeds/suspects.csv` is read from the repo, so editing that CSV on `main` is its only upkeep.
- A new mart column the page reads must also be in `tests/fixtures/marts/`, or the page tests fail. `food_verdicts.parquet`, `price_changes_yearly.parquet`, `growth_since_2018.parquet`, `change_only_monthly.parquet` (with `month` cast to `timestamp[ms]`) and `seasonal_baseline.parquet` (with `latest_month` and `recent_month` cast likewise) there are whole marts, copied from a local build; `farmer_share_monthly.parquet` keeps the months from Sep 2025 plus national red onion in 2023 for the hook. The page tests pin their numbers.
