- Red and white onions reached P600 a kilo in Manila-area markets until Monday 9 January 2023, which was about double the price of chicken and 25% to 50% more than beef, per DA market monitoring. — Gulf News, https://gulfnews.com/world/asia/philippines/philippines-onion-prices-now-higher-than-meat-heres-why-1.1673378206162, undated in fetch (January 2023; page refers to 9 and 11 January). ## The chain

- Benguet vegetables pass through 3 to 8 intermediaries before reaching final consumers, and each player incurs costs and gets paid for labour and capital. — Benguet State University, Informing Policy and Practice Policy Brief No. 2022-9 (Launio et al.), https://bsu.edu.ph/wp-content/uploads/IPP/2022-9-Why-Prices-of-Vegetables-in-Manila-are-Double-or-Triple-the-Prices-Received-by-Farmers-in-Benguet.pdf, 2022. Primary (university policy brief; terminal report to DA-BAR).
- The brief estimates the average total marketing margin (retail price minus farm-received price) for highland vegetables at P44 to P124 a kilo for Benguet to Manila. — same BSU brief, 2022. Primary.
- In the brief's main chain, four intermediaries link a Benguet farmer and a Manila consumer: a disposer/assembler (negotiates with buyers, sorts, grades and loads), a wholesaler-buyer (packs and trucks from the trading post to Manila), a terminal wholesaler (sells on to retailers) and a retailer. Per-kilo costs of packaging and transport are listed at each step. — same BSU brief, 2022. Primary.
- Most vegetable supply chains have a "trader, or a viajero or middle man who links the growers to the market or to the end buyers." — PIDS Discussion Paper 2012-08, Llanto, Sombilla and Quimba, "Interregional Trade of High-value Fruits and Vegetables: Issues on Transport and Shipping", https://pidswebs.pids.gov.ph/CDN/PUBLICATIONS/pidsdps1208.pdf, 2012-03. Primary. Old paper, preliminary series.
- That PIDS paper found transport costs and an allowance for losses to be the major cost items for traders (viajeros) and retailers; transport was about 30% of total trading cost for papaya traders and about 10% for lettuce and tomato. — same PIDS paper, 2012-03. Primary.
- Market fees exist at the wholesale-retail level: in Naga public market, traders without permanent stalls paid P10 a day for up to 10 bags and P20 for more. — UPLB Center for Strategic Planning and Policy Studies Working Paper 2018-08 (Chupungco), https://pidswebs.pids.gov.ph/CDN/PUBLICATIONS/cspps_working_paper_2018-08.pdf, 2018 (year from the working paper number; the paper does not otherwise show a date in what I read). Primary, but one market only; use as an example, not a national figure.
- In the BSU brief, second-level wholesalers and retailers get the highest net margin but handle less volume and carry more risk; disposers earn under P2 a kilo commission on volume crops. — BSU brief above, 2022. Primary.
- The PSA farm-to-retail gap for red onion (red creole) was P117.28 a kilo in 2023, the largest of the crops listed, with potato at P74.44. — PSA, Agricultural Prices and Marketing 2019-2023, https://psa.gov.ph/system/files/main-publication/AFIS-Prices-and-Marketing_2019-2023_SIGNED.pdf (403 to fetcher; figures read via search summary of the page), undated (2023 values preliminary). Primary but not opened. PSA defines the gap as retail price minus farm price. It does not split the gap by trader, wholesaler or retailer.
- Postharvest losses: a SEARCA study funded by ADB found 63.90% loss for red onion moved from Bongabon, Nueva Ecija to Divisoria (cold stored), 30.85% for mango (Pangasinan to Manila) and 24.14% for tomato (Bukidnon to Manila). — Manila Bulletin, https://mb.com.ph/2022/6/17/invest-in-post-harvest-facilities-to-improve-agri-productivity (read via https://www.searca.org/press/invest-post-harvest-facilities-improve-agri-productivity), 2022-06-17. Secondary: study itself not reached; the mango peso value in the article looks like a typo, so do not quote peso values.
- Benguet cabbage spoilage: the 2019 JESAM paper's abstract says vegetable harvests in the Philippines are wasted due to spoilage "(~42%)", and its body also says 20% to 50% is lost during marketing. — "Solid waste management and reduction of agricultural post-harvest losses using cold-storage: perceptions of farmers in Benguet, Philippines", Journal of Environmental Science and Management, pp. 99-108, https://ovcre.uplb.edu.ph/journals-uplb/index.php/JESAM/article/download/89/69 (403 to fetcher; read via search summary and the UPLB repository listing https://www.ukdr.uplb.edu.ph/journal-articles/5532), June 2019 per the repository. Primary but not opened. Correction to the seed: the 42% is for vegetable harvests in the Philippines in general, not Benguet cabbage specifically; the body reportedly says 44%. Use "about 42%" with "vegetable harvest" wording.
- A cold-storage and trucking cost breakdown in pesos for onions: Not confirmed. Tried the PSA report (403), the Manila Bulletin 2018 logistics piece https://mb.com.ph/2018/02/15/agricultural-logistics-in-the-philippines (403) and PhilMech (no single official loss table surfaced). Nothing I read states what a fair middle cut is in percent.
- PhilMech loss range: PhilMech is cited as putting postharvest losses across major commodities at 10% to 50% (read via search summary only, no page opened). Not confirmed from PhilMech itself.
- Lawmakers said in May 2024 that the gap between farmgate and retail prices is wide and intermediaries capture much of it; a House trade panel chair said supermarket profit margin is about 8% (10% for frozen goods). The "as much as 200 percent" profit claim is attributed to unnamed "administration lawmakers" with no data. — Philstar, https://philstar.com/headlines/2024/05/03/2352191/traders-middlemen-blamed-soaring-commodity-prices, 2024-05-03. Secondary: no primary reachable. Do not use the 200 percent figure.

## Why it matters

- Poverty incidence among farmers was 27.0% and among fisherfolk 27.4% in 2023, against 15.5% for the whole population, with fisherfolk and farmers second and third highest after Indigenous Peoples (32.4%). — PSA, 2023 Poverty Statistics for Basic Sectors, released 2025 (one summary gives 31 March 2025), reported at https://malaya.com.ph/business/business-news/ph-poverty-rates-highest-among-fisherfolk-farmers-indigenous-groups (403) and https://www.bworldonline.com/?p=663031 (403); figures read via search summaries only. PSA pages tried and blocked (403): https://psa.gov.ph/content/poverty-incidence-declined-2021-2023-ten-basic-sectors , https://psa.gov.ph/statistics/poverty/node/1684075985 , https://psa.gov.ph/system/files/phdsd/Press%20Release_2023%20Full%20Year%20Poverty%20Statistics.pdf . Secondary: no primary opened. Re-verify on the PSA page before publishing.
- Fisherfolk poverty was 30.2% in 2021 (read via search summary of the PSA table). So both sectors fell, but still sit about 12 points above the national 15.5%.
- National poverty incidence for 2025 is 9.7% of the population (about 11.08 million people), released 21 August 2026 and updated 26 August 2026, down from 15.5% in 2023. — GMA News, https://www.gmanetwork.com/news/money/economy/1000195/poverty-rate-declined-across-all-regions-in-ph-in-2025-psa/story/, 2026-08-28; also Manila Bulletin https://mb.com.ph/2026/08/21/philippines-hits-lowest-poverty-rate-in-recorded-history, 2026-08-21 (403, summary only). Secondary: PSA release not opened.
- Not confirmed: 2025 poverty rates for farmers and fisherfolk. I found no 2025 basic-sector release; PSA's technical notes reportedly say the basic-sector series is released every two years after the full-year statistics. Tried PSA poverty pages (403) and the GMA article (does not mention the sectors). The 2023 sector figures are the latest I could find. ## Who got caught

- Not 100% matched to seed. PCC found six firms (and responsible officers) liable for an onion cartel involving market allocation across importation, cold storage and wholesale distribution of red and yellow (or white) onions, under Section 14(b)(2) of the Philippine Competition Act, and fined them a combined P1.76 billion, the largest under the Act. — Philippine Competition Commission press release, https://www.phcc.gov.ph/resource-details/pcc-finds-onion-importers-traders-liable-for-cartel-behavior-slaps-record-fine, 2026-10-15. Primary.
  - The six liable parties named in the ruling: La Reina Fresh Vegetables & Young Indoor Plants, Inc.; Yom Trading Corp.; Vegefru Producing Store; Rosal Fruit and Vegetable Trading; Golden Shine International Freight Forwarders Corp.; Tian Long Corp. Fines were P330 million each for the first five and P118.33 million for Tian Long. (These add to about P1.77 billion, so the P1.76 billion headline is the PCC's rounding; quote the PCC total.) — same release.
  - Fines were computed from actual import clearance volumes and PSA wholesale prices because the importers' audited financial statements were found grossly understated, and trebled automatically because onion is a prime commodity. — same release.
  - The respondents "collectively controlled nearly 50 percent of all approved import clearances" in 2020 and 2021; a court-ordered dawn raid on 6 September 2023 produced evidence. — same release; GMA News, https://www.gmanetwork.com/news/money/economy/1006080/pcc-fines-onion-importers-with-record-p1-76-billion-fine/story/, 2026-10-16.
  - Two other respondents (Philippine Vieva Group of Companies and the Vegetable Importers, Exporters & Vendors Association of the Philippines) were cleared for insufficient evidence. — same PCC release.
  - Seed row: needs rewording to "The Philippine Competition Commission found that six onion importers, traders, a freight forwarder and a cold-storage firm divided up the onion trade, and fined them a combined P1.76 billion, the largest fine under the Competition Act.   - Newer development: MLex reported that, as of 19 October 2026, all respondents except one sole proprietor, declared in default, had filed motions for reconsideration; those denied can go to the Court of Appeals, and the PCC said it would coordinate with the DOJ on possible criminal prosecution under Section 30 of the Act. — MLex, https://www.mlex.com/mlex/articles/2539324/philippine-onion-cartel-respondents-challenge-record-28m-fine (403, read via search summary), 2026-10. ## What can be done

- The PCC can investigate on its own initiative, on a verified complaint or on referral; issue subpoenas; and, with a court order, inspect business premises and records. — Republic Act 10667 (Philippine Competition Act), Sections 12, 31 and 33, https://lawphil.net/statutes/repacts/ra2015/ra_10667_2015.html, 2015. Primary (Lawphil copy; Official Gazette copy not opened).
- Under the Act, the PCC can impose administrative fines of up to P100 million for a first offense and P100 million to P250 million for a second offense on prohibited agreements such as price fixing and market division (Section 14), and fines are trebled for prime commodities per the PCC's onion release. Criminal penalty for cartel agreements is 2 to 7 years imprisonment and a fine of P50 million to P250 million, imposed on responsible officers (Section 30). — RA 10667 Sections 29 and 30, Lawphil link above; the trebling is from the PCC release above. Primary.
- To complain to the PCC: use the E-Complaint Portal at https://ccms.phcc.gov.ph or file a verified complaint (name, address, email, contact number; who is complained of; the acts alleged; supporting documents; and a verification). Contact: queries@phcc.gov.ph, (+632) 8771-9PCC (722), 9/F and 25/F Vertis North Corporate Center 1, North Avenue, Quezon City. — PCC, https://phcc.gov.ph/enforcement/file-a-complaint, undated, read 2026-11-20. Primary. The portal launch was reported by Manila Bulletin 2026-01-05 (https://mb.com.ph/2026/01/05/antitrust-watchdog-streamlines-complaint-system, not opened).
- DA describes KADIWA ng Pangulo as a "direct marketing platform designed by the Department of Agriculture" giving consumers access to quality, safe food and farm producers a ready market; farmers, fisherfolk and micro, small and medium enterprises sell at outlets without paying rent. — DA, https://www.da.gov.ph/govt-ramps-up-kadiwa-ng-pangulo-to-provide-affordable-food-items-increase-incomes-of-farmers-fisherfolk/, 2023-03-23. Primary.
- DA says KADIWA ng Pangulo directly markets farm and fishery products from small-scale producers, covers rice, vegetables, meat and fish, and "by cutting out middlemen" aims to lower costs for consumers; it targets 1,500 stores by 2028. — DA, https://www.da.gov.ph/da-phlpost-partner-to-expand-kadiwa-network/, 2025-04-04. Primary.
- Not confirmed: the name "Kadiwa ng Bagong Bayaning Pilipino". I found no DA page using it; the DA pages found use "KADIWA ng Pangulo" (and earlier "Kadiwa ni Ani at Kita", launched 13 September 2019, https://www.da.gov.ph/kadiwa-ni-ani-at-kita-project-launched/). Use "KADIWA ng Pangulo" unless you can cite the other name.
- DTI consumer hotline: 1-384 (One-DTI) and ConsumerCare@dti.gov.ph, per DTI pages summarised in search results; DTI also points to the Philippine Online Dispute Resolution System, https://podrs.dti.gov.ph/. — DTI, https://dti.gov.ph/consumers/complaints (www and non-www versions returned 404 / DNS failure; read via search summary), undated. Not confirmed from a fetched DTI page; hours conflict across sources (DTI archive says 8 am to 5 pm daily, a law-firm site says 24/7), so omit hours. Tried https://www.dti.gov.ph/archives/news-archives/dti-launches-consumer-corner-1-dti-hotline (404).
- DA price complaints: I found no DA hotline number confirmed. DA has urged consumers to report price concerns with the #DABantayPresyo hashtag (search summary, January 2025) and its pages link the 8888 Citizen's Complaint Hotline, but the DA articles I read do not say 8888 is for price complaints. — DA, https://www.da.gov.ph/da-cracks-down-on-overpriced-onions-pork-as-retailers-flout-msrp/, 2025-12-17, and https://www.da.gov.ph/da-to-step-up-price-monitoring-to-keep-food-prices-fair-stable/, 2026-01-09. Primary for what is on the page; Not confirmed as a DA price-complaint channel.
- RA 12022, the Anti-Agricultural Economic Sabotage Act (approved 26 September 2024), defines smuggling (P10 million combined value), hoarding, profiteering and cartel of agricultural and fishery products as economic sabotage; penalties for the main offenses are life imprisonment and a fine of five times the value of the products. Under Section 21 any person may file a verified criminal complaint with the DOJ Special Team of Prosecutors, or an administrative complaint with the proper agency. It sets no hotline. — Republic Act 12022, https://lawphil.net/statutes/repacts/ra2024/ra_12022_2024.html, 2024-09-26. Primary (Lawphil; Official Gazette and Senate copies not opened). Note the cartel definition is "two or more competitors in the same market" agreeing to coordinated acts that artificially manipulate supply or prices; profiteering is defined by price at least 10% above the Daily Price Index after an abnormal situation is declared.
